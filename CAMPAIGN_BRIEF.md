# lululemon — "POWER MOVES"
### Campaign Creative Brief

---

## 1. The Problem We're Solving

lululemon built a category — then got typecast by it. In culture, the brand is shorthand for a specific caricature: the "soccer mom" running errands in Aligns, dressed for a workout she isn't doing. It's a joke on TikTok, a punchline in sitcoms, a $128 legging that never sees a gym.

That caricature undersells the actual customer. The real lululemon buyer is disproportionately ambitious, working, and *actually* training — she (and increasingly he) just also happens to run a P&L, close deals, or run a household like a second job. The brand's image has drifted toward leisure. Her life is anything but leisurely.

**The gap:** Culture sees lululemon as athleisure for people who aren't athletes. We need it to be seen as performance wear for people whose day is the competition — on the mat and in the meeting.

---

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary:** Women 28–45, career-driven (manager to executive track), training 3–5x/week (strength, running, studio), high income, time-poor. She does not see "fitness" and "career" as separate identities — she sees them as the same discipline applied twice a day.

**Secondary:** Men in the same category, a growing and underleveraged share of lululemon's base — reinforces the brand is not gendered-niche, it's performance-niche.

**Psychographic in one line:** *She trains like she's got something to prove, because at work, she does.*

---

## 3. The Insight

> "People don't clock out of ambition to work out, and they don't clock out of discipline when they leave the gym. It's the same person, same intensity, different room."

The world keeps trying to split her in two — the "wellness girl" and the "girlboss," the gym self and the work self. She's not two people managing two identities. She's one person operating at one standard, all day.

---

## 4. The Big Idea

**lululemon doesn't dress you for rest. It dresses you for the parts of your day that actually demand something from you — the workout AND the walk into the room where you have to perform.**

We stop showing product only in the studio. We follow the *same woman* from a 6am lift session straight into a 9am pitch, a board meeting, a client dinner — same energy, same posture, same standard, no wardrobe-mandated personality change required.

---

## 5. Campaign Name & Tagline

**Campaign:** POWER MOVES

**Master tagline:**
> **Every power move starts before 9AM.**

**Supporting line:**
> Trained like an athlete. Dressed like it, everywhere else, too.

**Alt taglines (for testing):**
- "Built for the set. Built for the boardroom."
- "Strong suit. Literally."
- "Off the clock never looked like on."
- "One standard. Every room."

---

## 6. Messaging Pillars

1. **Same person, no wardrobe change.** Product performs identically whether the "workout" is a workout or a ten-hour day that started with one.
2. **Discipline is discipline.** The mental reps of training (showing up, pushing through, resetting after a loss) are the same reps that build a career. We say this out loud instead of implying it.
3. **Retire the caricature.** Directly, wittily push back on the "not a real athlete" stereotype — self-aware, not defensive.
4. **Built for movement you can't predict.** Fabric/fit story: engineered for a body that goes from a deadlift to a redeye to a keynote in the same 24 hours.

---

## 7. Tone & Visual Direction

- **Tone:** Confident, dry-witted, a little confrontational toward the stereotype — never earnest wellness-speak, never "girlboss" cringe. Think: the calm of someone who has already won the argument.
- **Visual grammar:** One continuous take or hard-cut match: gym rig / conference table shot with identical camera angle, identical framing, identical lighting temperature — visually asserting "this is the same scene."
- **Casting:** Real professionals with real training regimens (not just fitness influencers) — surgeons, founders, litigators, athletes-turned-executives. Casting *is* the proof.
- **Color/mood:** Keep lululemon's clean minimalism; introduce a warmer, richer palette shift for "boardroom" scenes vs. cool tones for "training" scenes, unified by identical composition — visual proof it's one story, two rooms.

**This isn't hypothetical — the culture already exists, we just have to point at it:**
- lululemon's own **"The Lab"** training events already cast real, visibly strong men and women training together as a community, not a solo influencer shoot. That's our casting brief, not a mood board.
- Street style already put a **blazer over a sports bra with tailored joggers and heels** — the exact "boardroom + active" silhouette — on the street, unprompted. This becomes a literal hero look, not a metaphor.
- Run clubs (5:30am, dark, city streets, a crowd of them) are already normal. We don't have to invent the "athlete who also works" — we have to stop cutting her out of the ad.

---

## 8. Executions

| Channel | Concept |
|---|---|
| **Hero film (:60)** | Split-screen / match-cut anthem spot following 3 real women through one day: gym → commute → boardroom → gym, in that loop, scored to a single unbroken beat. |
| **Social (:15 vertical)** | "A Day In Two Rooms" — rapid-fire cuts of the same outfit-adjacent movement (a lunge / a stride into a room; a plank hold / a hard stare across a table). |
| **OOH / Transit** | Split diptych posters: gym photo | boardroom photo, same subject, same pose mirrored. Headline: "Same person." |
| **Retail** | In-store looks styled as "6AM" and "9AM" on the same mannequin pair — the product doesn't change, the room does. |
| **Influencer/UGC** | #PowerMoves prompt: user duets showing their own gym-to-work transition, no outfit change. |
| **Earned/PR** | A direct, quotable statement addressing the "soccer mom" stereotype head-on in founder/CMO press — reframes it as a badge, not an insult. |

## 10. Success Metrics

- Shift in brand perception tracking: "is for real athletes" score, "is for busy professionals" score (pre/post campaign survey).
- Earned media sentiment on the stereotype-reframe PR moment.
- UGC volume on #PowerMoves.
- Conversion lift in career-adjacent product lines (blazers, "commute-to-class" convertible pieces) vs. pure studio product.
